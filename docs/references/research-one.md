Technical Physics Engine Specification for Zip-Line Engineering SimulatorsSection 1: Arbitration of ContradictionsC1 — Target SagThe existing reports express three distinct metrics that serve complementary engineering roles across the lifecycle of a zip-line span:Unloaded Initial Drape ($\approx 5\%$ of span): A field installation heuristic used during stringing. This tension setting ensures the unpopulated cable maintains sufficient baseline curvature, preventing over-tensioning prior to live-load application.Sag Floor Under Maximum Load ($\ge 2\%$ of span): A hard structural lower bound designed to cap horizontal anchor reactions. As sag $D \to 0$, horizontal tension $H \approx \frac{P \cdot L}{4D}$ approaches infinity. Operating below a $2\%$ sag ratio under peak live load risks yielding cable terminations, tree anchors, or structural posts.Loaded Sag Target ($\approx 8\%$ of span): An operational optimization target for standard commercial zip lines. An $8\%$ sag profile provides passive speed regulation, allowing gravity acceleration in the first half of the span and natural gravitational deceleration on the ascending exit slope, thereby mitigating terminal braking kinetic energy requirements.In commercial zip-line engineering under NF EN 15567-1, target sag is not defined by a single normative constant; it is an optimized design output constrained by clearance envelopes, maximum allowable anchor forces, and arrival velocity limits.Structural Verdict and Design RulesThe three values describe distinct physical quantities rather than a contradiction.
